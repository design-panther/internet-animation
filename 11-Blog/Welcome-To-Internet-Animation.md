# Welcome to Internet Animation

Somewhere on your phone right now is an app that greeted you with a tiny burst of confetti, a checkbox that morphed into a checkmark, or a loading spinner that pulsed just slowly enough to feel patient instead of broken. You didn't notice any of it — and that's the whole point. Good motion on the web is nearly invisible. It does a job, and then it gets out of the way. This course is about learning to make that kind of motion on purpose, instead of by accident.

That's the reframe I want you to sit with before we touch a single keyframe: **motion is interface, not decoration.** An animation is not a garnish you sprinkle on a finished design to make it feel "modern." It's a piece of communication with a job to do — announce a state change, guide attention, confirm an action, soften a wait — and like every piece of communication, it has an audience who is trying to get something done. If you can't say who that animation is for and what it's supposed to accomplish, it isn't ready, no matter how nice the easing curve looks. We'll spend sixteen weeks building the muscle to ask that question automatically, and then building the technical range to answer it in whatever format the job calls for.

## The shape of the semester

The course is built in four units, and they escalate on purpose. **Unit 1, Foundations** (Weeks 1–4), is where you learn to see: the landscape of vector animation, the mechanics of vector drawing, the classic principles of animation adapted for UI, and, critically, the User-Centered Design habits of research and intent that should precede any motion decision. It ends with Project 1. **Unit 2, The Lottie Pipeline** (Weeks 5–8), takes you into the industry-standard workflow: After Effects, the Bodymovin export, the Lottie/dotLottie format, and the real-world discipline of implementing and optimizing that motion so it actually ships. It ends with a midterm critique and Project 2. **Unit 3, Interactive & Alternative Platforms** (Weeks 9–12) is where things get responsive: Rive's state machines, a deep dive into Fancy Animation Studio, a full round of usability-testing motion with real users, and web-native SVG/CSS animation. It ends with Project 3 and your final project proposal. And **Unit 4, the Final Project** (Weeks 13–16), is yours — research, production, peer testing, iteration, and a real presentation of finished work.

If you want the granular version — what happens which week, what's due when — that lives on the [[02-Schedule/_index|16-week schedule]], and each week has its own page (start with [[Week-01]]) laying out topics, activities, and resources. The [[01-Syllabus/_index|syllabus]] is the contract underneath all of it: outcomes, grading, policies. Read it this week. I mean that literally — it's short, and everything else on this wiki assumes you have.

## How this wiki actually works

This isn't a static syllabus PDF you read once and forget. It's a working tool, and it's built to be used all semester:

- **Week pages** ([[Week-01]] and onward) are your weekly home base — topic, in-class activities, what's due, and curated resources for that specific stretch of material.
- **Project briefs**, like [[Exercise-1-Motion-Audit]], tell you exactly what you're making and exactly what it's graded on. Templates such as [[Motion-Brief-Template]] give you scaffolding so you're not reinventing the UCD paperwork every single project.
- **[[04-Resources/_index|Learning Resources]]** is a rated library of tutorials, docs, videos, and tools, organized by unit and week. Every link has been checked and every entry is free-first — when you're stuck at 11pm on a Bodymovin export error, this is where you go before you go anywhere else.
- **The class calendar** is the one piece of this wiki that isn't mine alone — it's shared, and on the class platform it's editable by *everyone in the class*. Find it in the sidebar under **⧗ Class Calendar**. Use it to schedule your own study sessions, flag a question for critique day, or fix a date that's drifted. Treat it as a group whiteboard for the semester's logistics, because that's exactly what it is.
- **Whiteboards** are the visual, collaborative canvases we'll use for critique sessions, moodboards, and working through a UCD problem live — a place to put sketches, screenshots, and sticky notes next to each other instead of describing them in a document.
- **This blog** — the [[11-Blog/_index|blog]] itself — is where each unit's big ideas get walked through slowly, in prose, with the library's best resources woven in. Read the unit essay in its first week; it's the connective tissue between the schedule and the library.

None of this is decoration either. It's infrastructure, built the same way we're asking you to build motion: for a specific user, doing a specific job.

## How to actually succeed in here

Start now. **Exercise 1, the motion audit** ([[Exercise-1-Motion-Audit]]), is due Friday of Week 1, and it is deliberately not a big lift — it's designed to get you looking at motion critically from day one, before you've written a line of code or opened After Effects. Don't let "it's early, I have time" turn into a Thursday-night scramble. The habit you build in Week 1 is the habit you'll be running on in Week 16.

Come to the work sessions. ET 206 is open Tuesday and Thursday, 8:00–9:15am, and it's not mandatory in the sense of roll call — it's mandatory in the sense that this is a studio course, and studio courses are won in the room, not in isolation. Bring your in-progress file, not your finished one. That's where I'm most useful to you.

And treat critique as material, not as a performance review. When your classmates or I look at your work and ask "who is this for, and how do you know it's working for them," that question is not a gotcha — it's the entire discipline of User-Centered Design compressed into one sentence, and you'll be asking it of yourself, unprompted, by Unit 3. Motion that survives contact with a real audience is the only kind worth shipping. Everything else is just something moving on a screen.

Welcome to the semester. Let's make things move on purpose.
