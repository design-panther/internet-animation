# Grading

Grades are based primarily on the quality of your completed work: how well the
piece fulfills the assignment, imagination, originality, visual/verbal
expression, attendance, enthusiasm, effort and improvement — and, in this
course, the quality of your **User-Centered Design rationale**: can you say who
the motion is for, what it's supposed to do, and how you know it works?

## The in-class exercises

Fourteen exercises are offered across the semester, in class. You choose which
to complete — **any seven count**, at 10 points each. Completing more than
seven doesn't add points, but it does add skill. Exercise instructions are
given in class and posted to the [[03-Projects/_index|Projects & exercises]]
section as they're assigned.

## Letter-grade scale

Standard 90/80/70/60% cutoffs on 270 points:

| Grade | Points | Percent |
|---|---|---|
| A | 243–270 | 90%+ |
| B | 216–242 | 80–89% |
| C | 189–215 | 70–79% |
| D | 162–188 | 60–69% |
| F | below 162 | <60% |

## What each grade means

**A — Superior.** Scholarship strong, exceeding the requirements of the course.
Contributions exceed the assignment, showing independent resourcefulness.
Positive benefit to class; cooperation productive, constant, spontaneous;
improvement marked and growing.

**B — Good, above average.** Accurate and complete, meeting all requirements of
the course and beyond. Good initiative when stimulated; attitude proper and
beneficial to the group; showing marks of progress.

**C — Average.** Requirements for course work met on time, reflecting a
demonstrated grasp of concepts and reasonable participation, with evidence of
needing encouragement. Initiative uncertain; cooperation irregular.

**D — Below average.** Some requirements unmet; initiative lacking; attitude
indifferent or resistant to engagement with course content; improvement not
noticeable.

**F.** Most of the requirements have not been met.
