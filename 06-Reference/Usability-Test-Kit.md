# The Usability Test Kit

The Week-11 method, reused in Week 14 and available any time you want real
answers instead of your own opinion. Three users, twenty minutes, three test
types. Classmates count as users (better: one classmate, one non-designer
friend, one person over 40).

## Ground rules

- **You are testing the motion, not the user.** Say so out loud.
- **Don't explain first.** The whole point is what they perceive unaided.
- **Write down what they say verbatim** — synthesis comes later.
- **n=3 finds problems; it doesn't prove success.** Report findings as observations ("2 of 3 hesitated"), never as statistics.

## Test 1 — Comprehension ("what just happened?")

Show the animation in context, once. Ask: *"What just happened?"* then *"What
would you do next?"* If they can't say what the motion communicated, the
motion isn't communicating.

## Test 2 — Preference (A/B, but ask why)

Two versions (e.g. two easings, with/without overshoot, 200ms vs 400ms). Show
both, alternate order between users. Ask which they prefer **and why** — the
"why" is the finding; the vote is just a tiebreaker.

## Test 3 — Timing ("too slow, too fast, or fine?")

Play the animation in a realistic task loop *three times in a row* (the third
repetition is where charming becomes annoying). Ask: too slow, too fast, or
fine? Watch for the wince.

## Synthesis (one page)

1. **What you tested** — piece, versions, the three users (role, device).
2. **Findings** — 3–5 observations with verbatim quotes.
3. **The one revision** — the single change the findings most clearly demand.
4. **What you're NOT changing and why** — findings you judged noise; defend the call.
