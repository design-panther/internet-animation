# Exercise 1 — Motion Audit · 10 pts · due Week 1

Find **three animations in apps you actually use** and analyze who they serve
and why. This is the course's thesis in miniature: motion has an audience and
a job, and you can learn to see both.

## Deliverable

A short document (or slide set) with, for each of the three animations:

1. **Where it lives** — app, screen, trigger (screen recording or GIF strongly encouraged).
2. **Who it serves** — the user in that moment: what were they doing, what did they need?
3. **The job** — feedback? orientation? delight? masking a wait? Which, and how do you know?
4. **Verdict** — does it help or decorate? Would you cut it, keep it, or change it?

## Tips

- "Boring" utility animations (a spinner, a pull-to-refresh, a tab transition) are often richer to analyze than showpieces.
- If you can't say what the animation is *for*, that's a finding — write it down.
- Look at one animation with **Reduce Motion** turned on in your phone's accessibility settings. What changes?

## Grading (10 pts)

- 6 pts — three animations, each with all four parts
- 2 pts — specificity (screens, triggers, users — not generalities)
- 2 pts — quality of the verdicts (an argument, not a vibe)
