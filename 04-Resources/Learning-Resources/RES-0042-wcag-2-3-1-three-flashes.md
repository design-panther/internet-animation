## What it covers
The official W3C explanation of WCAG Success Criterion 2.3.1 (Level A), the core seizure-safety rule: web content must not flash more than three times in any one-second period, or the flash must be below the general and red-flash thresholds, unless the flashing area is small enough (no more than 25% of any 10-degree visual field). It explains that flashing content can trigger migraines, dizziness, nausea, and seizures in people with photosensitive seizure disorders, and that red flashes require extra caution.

## What you'll learn
- The exact flash-rate and flash-area thresholds that make content "safe"
- Why red flashing is treated as a special, higher-risk case
- How to reason about strobing/flickering effects in exported animation (e.g., rapid color-cycling or glitch effects)
- The medical stakes behind this specific, testable rule
