## What it covers
A Nielsen Norman Group article that lays out exactly when motion helps a UI (feedback on system state, signaling state changes, clarifying navigation/hierarchy, showing how an element behaves) versus when it hurts (distraction, attention-hijacking "dark pattern" countdowns, cognitive overload from too many simultaneous animations).

## What you'll learn
- Four concrete, named jobs motion can do well: feedback, state-change signaling, navigation clarity, and signifiers
- Specific failure modes: gratuitous decoration, distraction, and manipulative attention-hijacking
- Why "light touch" is the article's core recommendation — motion should support usability, not decorate it
- A checklist mindset for evaluating whether a proposed animation earns its place in an interface
