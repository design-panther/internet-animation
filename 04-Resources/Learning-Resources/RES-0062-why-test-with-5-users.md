## What it covers
Nielsen's classic 2000 argument (with math from his study with Thomas Landauer) that a single test user reveals about 31% of a design's usability problems, with diminishing returns from each additional user — so running several small 5-user tests across design iterations beats one large 15-user study.

## What you'll learn
- The math behind why small-sample tests still surface most problems
- Why iterating with 3-5 users between redesigns beats one big study
- When you DO need more than 5 users (multiple distinct user groups)
- How to budget/plan a semester project's usability round around a small panel

## Notes
Directly supports the course's "running small 3-person tests" method for Week 11 — gives students the research rationale for why a 3-person animation preference/comprehension test is a legitimate, defensible method rather than a corner-cut.
