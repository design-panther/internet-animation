## What it covers
A practical chapter on first-click testing: giving a participant a task, showing them a design, and stopping the moment they click — then measuring whether that first click was on the right path. It cites the finding that users who click correctly the first time complete tasks 2-3x more often than those who click incorrectly, and walks through setting objectives, writing tasks, recruiting, and reading heatmap results.

## What you'll learn
- Why the first click is such a strong predictor of overall task success
- How to write a good first-click test task/scenario
- How to read a click heatmap and turn it into design decisions
- How to adapt the method to test whether a motion cue (e.g., an animated affordance) correctly draws the eye/click to the right spot

## Notes
Supports Week 11's "first-click ... style methods adapted to motion" — useful when testing whether an animated hint, hover state, or transition successfully directs a user's first interaction.
