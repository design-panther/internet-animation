## What it covers
Written by the author of "Designing Interface Animation," this article explains vestibular disorders and motion sensitivity in plain language, identifies which kinds of web animation are most likely to trigger discomfort (large-scale movement, parallax, spinning/zooming), and lays out concrete design strategies — including `prefers-reduced-motion` — for shipping motion responsibly.

## What you'll learn
- What vestibular disorders are and why certain animations (not all) trigger real physical symptoms
- Which specific animation patterns (parallax scroll, simulated 3D, large-scale zoom/spin) are highest-risk
- Concrete design alternatives to risky animation patterns that keep the interaction but reduce harm
- How to think about motion sensitivity as a User-Centered Design constraint, not an afterthought
